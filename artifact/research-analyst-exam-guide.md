# SEBI Research Analyst Exam (NISM Series XV) — Complete Guide

Exported from the Claude doc of the same name, 4 October 2026.

## Quick overview

The "SEBI Research Analyst exam" is officially the **NISM-Series-XV: Research Analyst Certification Examination**: a 100-mark, 2-hour computer-based test that costs ₹1,500 including GST, needs 60 marks to pass, and gives a certificate valid for 3 years.

- **Who runs it:** NISM (National Institute of Securities Markets), a public trust set up by SEBI. SEBI sets the rule; NISM conducts the exam.
- **Why it exists:** Under Regulation 7(2) of the SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014, anyone who prepares or publishes research reports as a registered RA, or works for one, must pass it.
- **Latest change:** The syllabus and pattern were revised from 20 January 2026: 15 chapters, with 5 case studies (20 marks) now in the paper.
- **Important:** Passing the exam is not the licence. To sell or publish research as a registered RA you still apply to SEBI separately (details below).

This exam is the entry gate. Think of it as the driving test: it proves you know the rules of the road, not that you can drive in Mumbai traffic.

## Who can take it, and who can register

Anyone can enrol for the exam, but it is mandatory for individuals who prepare or publish research for a registered RA or research entity, and for partners doing the same ([NISM FAQ](https://www.nism.ac.in/frequently-asked-questions-research-analyst)). Registering with SEBI as an individual Research Analyst has its own conditions, listed below.

- **Qualification:** After SEBI's December 2024 amendment, a graduate degree in finance, accountancy, business management, commerce, economics, capital markets, banking, insurance, actuarial science or other financial services is enough, and the old experience requirement was removed ([CS Kruti summary](https://cskruti.com/all-about-the-latest-sebi-amendment-regulations-for-research-analysts/)).
- **Certification:** A valid NISM-Series-XV certificate (Regulation 7(2)).
- **Capital requirement:** The old ₹1 lakh net worth rule for individuals was replaced by a graded deposit of ₹1–10 lakh based on client count, per a secondary summary ([Vikas Gupta](https://lawyervikasgupta.com/blog/sebi-ria-ra-compliance-changes-in-2025/)). I did not confirm the exact slabs in SEBI's primary text.
- **Open point:** Some 2025 summaries say the rules were widened to graduates of any discipline. I could not confirm that in the primary regulation, so check SEBI's current Regulation 7 before applying.

## Exam pattern

The paper is 100 marks in 2 hours, and you need 60 to pass. Since 20 January 2026, a fifth of it is case-based: 5 case studies replaced the earlier 2, per [NISM's circular](https://www.nism.ac.in/circular/nism-certification-nism-series-xv-research-analyst-certification-examination-2025-dated-december-05-2025/) and a [coaching-institute summary](https://www.profsheetalkunderacademy.com/blog/nism-xv-research-analyst-jan%202026-syllabus-updates).

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Questions | 100: 80 MCQs of 1 mark each, plus 5 case studies with 4 one-mark questions each |
| Total marks | 100 |
| Duration | 2 hours (120 minutes), about 72 seconds per question |
| Passing score | 60 marks (60%) |
| Negative marking | 25% of a mark per wrong answer, per [NISM's official exam table](https://www.nism.ac.in/certifications) |
| Mode | Computer-based at a NISM test centre; you pick the centre, date and slot |
| Certificate validity | 3 years from the exam date |
| Renewal | Pass the XV-B renewal exam before expiry; an expired certificate cannot be renewed |
| Re-evaluation | Not allowed; doubts about a question must be raised at the centre before you leave |
| On the day | Admit card plus an original ID (PAN, driving licence, passport or Aadhaar) 30 minutes early, per an older third-party page; confirm on the portal |

Source for the table: [NISM FAQ](https://www.nism.ac.in/frequently-asked-questions-research-analyst).

## Where and how the exam happens

You book the exam on NISM's certification portal, then sit it at a NISM test centre you choose from the portal, with a date and time slot of your choice ([NISM FAQ](https://www.nism.ac.in/frequently-asked-questions-research-analyst)). NISM's [exam centres page](https://www.nism.ac.in/exam-centres/) lets you schedule an exam anywhere in India and publishes a monthly test calendar; the October 2026 calendar was available there when I checked.

1. Register at [cert.nism.ac.in](https://cert.nism.ac.in/) and pay the ₹1,500 fee.
2. Pick a test centre, date and slot. I could not load the live centre list, so check the portal dropdown for the centres nearest you.
3. Download the admit card and take it, with an original photo ID, to the centre.
4. Take the 2-hour computer-based test.
5. Give your PAN during registration or later from your dashboard. The certificate then arrives within about two weeks of the exam.

NISM's official exam table marks only a few exams as remote-proctored (such as XII, XXIV and XXV-A/B), and Series XV is not one of them, so plan on a physical centre.

## Scope and career options

Passing the exam makes you eligible to work as a research analyst in India's regulated research space; it does not hand you a job. The certificate is India-specific and exists only to meet SEBI's Regulation 7(2).

- **Employed analyst:** At a brokerage, merchant bank, research house or similar entity. You do not register with SEBI yourself, but you must hold the NISM certificate, and the trading restrictions apply to you ([SEBI FAQ](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1418122025732.pdf)).
- **Independent SEBI-registered RA:** You register with SEBI and run your own research service. SEBI reports roughly 1,400 registered RAs according to a [third-party guide](https://www.aktai.app/blog/sebi-research-analyst-registration-guide-2026), so the field is small.
- **Adjacent roles:** Proxy advisers also register under the RA Regulations. NISM launched a separate NISM-Series-XXV-A exam in 2026 for staff associated with research services in sales and other non-core roles ([NISM circular list](https://www.nism.ac.in/frequently-asked-questions-research-analyst)).
- **Not the same as an Investment Adviser:** An RA publishes general research reports, while an IA gives personalised advice to a client ([comparison](https://taxationconsultancy.in/sebi-research-analyst-eligibility/)).

The exam is the entry gate, so what matters after it is your track record and the quality of your reports. Mutual funds, credit rating agencies and portfolio managers are exempt from registering as RAs but must follow the RA conduct rules when they publish research.

## What a research analyst actually does (the friend version)

A research analyst is paid to answer one question: "Is this company's stock worth the price it is trading at?" Then they write the answer down, with the reasoning, so investors can act on it.

Think of buying a second-hand bike. You don't pay the first price you hear. You check the service history, compare it with similar bikes, and ask whether the price matches what you will get. A research analyst does the same for a company, except the "bike" earns profits, owes money and has a management team that may or may not be telling the full story.

**How the work flows, step by step**

1. **Economy first:** Check growth, inflation, interest rates and policy, because they push every company around. NISM calls the full sequence E-I-C (economy, industry, company).
2. **Then the industry:** Is the industry growing, cyclical or being squeezed? Analysts use tools like Porter's five forces and PESTLE here.
3. **Then the company:** Read the business model, management quality and balance sheet, then check profit, cash flow and debt through ratios. Peer comparison tells you whether the numbers are good or just average.
4. **Then the valuation:** Turn all that into a fair-value estimate using DCF or multiples like P/E and EV/EBITDA, and compare it with the market price.
5. **Then the report:** Publish the view with a rating (Buy, Hold or Sell), a price target, the risks and the required disclosures.

**A typical week**

- Read results, filings and news the moment they land, and update the financial model with the new numbers.
- Listen to earnings calls and talk to management, customers, suppliers and competitors to test the story.
- Write and update reports, then explain the view to fund managers, clients or the sales desk.
- Keep the compliance trail clean: disclosures, conflicts of interest and personal-trading records.

Roughly speaking, it is mostly Excel, a lot of reading dull PDFs, and a small share of being confidently wrong in public. That last part is why the disclaimers exist.

**What makes it a regulated profession**

- SEBI defines a research analyst as the person primarily responsible for preparing or publishing a research report, giving a buy, sell or hold call, giving a price target, or giving an opinion on a public offer, whatever their job title ([SEBI FAQ](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1418122025732.pdf)).
- An analyst or their associate cannot trade the stock within 30 days before or 5 days after publishing a report on it.
- Buy, sell or hold calls on individual stocks based purely on charts also count. Only technical commentary on a whole sector or index is exempt, so "it's just my chart view" does not get you out of the rules.
- Independence is the point: the rules on trading, pay and disclosure exist so a Buy rating is not just the firm talking its own book.

## Study plan and tips

Spend your hours in proportion to the marks: Technical Analysis, Financial Analysis and Valuation together decide 39 of the 100 marks, and Legal and Regulatory adds 10. This plan is built on the weightage table above and assumes you already know basic finance, so it runs about three weeks.

1. **Week 1, rulebook and base:** Units 1–4 and Unit 14 (law and regulation). Learn the RA Regulations, the code of conduct and the trading restrictions cold, because they are easy marks.
2. **Week 2, analysis units:** Units 5, 6, 7, 9, 11 and 12. These are concept-heavy with a few formulas (returns, beta, Sharpe, Treynor).
3. **Week 3, the heavy hitters:** Units 8, 10 and 15. Practise ratios, DuPont, DCF and multiples by hand, then go through the chart patterns and indicators.
4. **Last 3–4 days:** Do the workbook's chapter-end questions and the official mock test, then redo the weak units. The 3 lightest units (1, 2, 3) are quick revision wins.

- **Use the latest workbook:** Pick the February 2026 edition or later; older PDFs cover the pre-revision syllabus.
- **Case studies:** The 5 case studies (20 marks) are applied calculations, so practise them under time.
- **Technical Analysis:** It is now the single biggest unit at 15 marks. If you already work with charts, you can bank marks quickly, but the paper tests the workbook's definitions (Dow Theory, pattern names), so skim it rather than skip it.
- **Negative marking:** Each wrong answer costs 25% of a mark, so guess only when you can eliminate at least one option.
- **Before booking:** Check the October 2026 test calendar, then pick a slot with a few days of buffer.
